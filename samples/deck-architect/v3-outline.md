# The design moves behind impeccable and superpowers

## Audience Model
- **Who's in the room:** Claude Code power users who have already shipped at least one of their own skills. They can read SKILL.md frontmatter without flinching. The hardest sell is the author of an established skill catalogue who thinks there is nothing here they haven't already seen. Second-hardest: the pragmatist who considers opinionated skills "over-engineering." `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they care about:** reliability (does the skill actually fire when it should?) and signal (when it fires, does it move the needle?). Token budget is a secondary concern they will raise under pressure. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they already know/believe:** how skills load; that keywords in `description` drive triggering; that long SKILL.md files cost context. They believe their skills are fine. They do not yet distinguish between skills that *instruct* and skills that *enforce*. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **Transformation (walk-in → walk-out):** "My skills are fine; I don't need to restructure them" → "I can name four concrete moves I'm going to apply to one skill this week, and I know how to tell if it backfired."
- **Likely objections / deep-dive triggers:**
  1. "Fair pick? Why these two and not the Anthropic-official skill library?"
  2. "The Superpowers Red Flags table is 12 rows. I am not doubling every SKILL.md."
  3. "How do I decide when to ban a behaviour versus trust the model's judgment?"
  4. "What does 'enforcement' even mean for a non-visual skill?"
  5. "If my new skill regresses trigger reliability, how fast do I find out?"

## Brief
- **Decision/ask:** before next Friday, pick one skill you own and apply two of the four moves this deck teaches. Track invocation count for 72 hours.
- **Format & time:** 12-minute live talk + ~5 min Q&A. Technical-conference register.
- **Constraints:** ~10 main slides + backup. Self-contained HTML deliverable. No corporate template.
- **One-sentence takeaway:** **The best skills aren't instructions for Claude — they are enforcement systems tuned to Claude's specific failure modes.**
- **Team contributors:** N/A — single-author analysis of two third-party skills.

## Narrative spine
**SCQA + Pyramid (Minto).** Power users don't need a survey; they need the answer up front with four MECE arguments behind it. Walking through "here is impeccable, here is superpowers" would lose them by slide 3.

## Cover slide

- **Subtitle (≤12 words):** *For Claude Code authors who already ship their own.*
- **Meta:** *Design-lessons talk · 12 min*

The "two skills everyone is copying" framing survives on slide 1's body copy where it belongs.

## Opening hook (first 30 seconds)
*"Most of your skills tell Claude what to do. The two most-installed community skills tell it what not to do — and one of them ships a command-line scanner that audits twenty-four anti-patterns without Claude running at all. That's the delta between a skill you wrote for yourself and a skill people install."*

Spoken, not displayed. Combines hook + STAR. The "impeccable ships a CLI" detail is the specific, surprising thing people will re-tell.

## STAR moment
Slide 2, the reframe: both skills look like capability additions; they are actually discipline-enforcement systems wrapped around the model's known failure modes. That reframe is what the audience quotes afterward. No manufactured second peak.

## Main flow

1. **Your skills mostly prescribe behaviour. The best ones proscribe it.**
   - Purpose: answer up front; set the POV on the first body slide
   - On-slide: the title sentence + four anchor phrases ("Name the anti-pattern," "Rebut the rationalization," "Ship a check that runs without Claude," "Decompose by failure mode")
   - Speaker notes: this is the thesis; the next eight slides defend each move with direct evidence from impeccable and superpowers. Slide 8 is the honest-costs slide. Slide 9 is the rollback. Slide 10 is the action. Do not promise a tool tour — the audience expects one; disarm them by not giving it.
   - Load: balanced
   - Visual: text-only

2. **Both skills are enforcement systems dressed as capability skills — and that's the real lesson.**
   - Purpose: the STAR reframe
   - On-slide: two-column contrast. Left column header "What people install them for" (struck-through: "better fonts," "a TDD workflow"). Right column header "What they actually do" (full-weight: "ban the fonts Claude reflexively picks," "pre-empt the excuses Claude uses to skip TDD").
   - Speaker notes: quote pbakaus on generic-template convergence; quote obra on "if you have a skill to do something, you must use it." Both authors are explicitly framing the model's defaults as the adversary. That framing is the design move, not the surface features.
   - Load: speaker-heavy
   - Visual: text-only two-column layout

3. **Vague guidance loses to strong priors. Name the specific wrong thing.**
   - Purpose: first concrete move, with proof from both skills
   - On-slide: two side-by-side examples. Impeccable: "DO NOT use Inter, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Space Grotesk…" Superpowers: "1% chance it applies means you MUST check."
   - Speaker notes: "use good fonts" activates nothing — the model's prior for "good font" is Inter. "Do not use Inter" forces a real decision. Generalizes to non-visual skills: deck-architect ships an explicit forbidden-phrase list for the same reason. Name the phrase, name the replacement.
   - Load: balanced
   - Visual: text-only

4. **Write the rebuttal before the model writes the rationalization.**
   - Purpose: the move that separates usable skills from great ones
   - On-slide: a four-row excerpt of the Superpowers Red Flags table. Left column: what the model is about to think. Right column: the rebuttal that fires first.
   - Speaker notes: the rationalizations the model reaches for under pressure are remarkably uniform across sessions. Observing yours once and writing the rebuttal inline is the single highest-leverage authoring move. This skill (deck-architect) has a `rationalizations.md` reference for the same reason.
   - Load: slide-heavy
   - Visual: stylized four-row table

5. **Ship a check that runs without Claude. Trust is a load-bearing feature.**
   - Purpose: the move that turns a skill into a system
   - On-slide: two artifacts — Impeccable's standalone auditor CLI (no AI runtime, usable in CI); Superpowers' subagent pressure-test methodology (validates a skill fires from a clean slate).
   - Speaker notes: instructions drift under model variance. Programmatic checks do not. The Impeccable CLI is the trust signal — anyone can run it and see the skill works. Mejba's controlled test picks up on this implicitly: the measured token reduction came from skills with teeth, not skills with suggestions. Your skill probably has zero external verification today.
   - Load: balanced
   - Visual: text-only

6. **Decompose by failure mode, not by topic.**
   - Purpose: explains the 18-command Impeccable surface and the Superpowers skill graph without sounding like a tool tour
   - On-slide: wrong way / right way. Wrong: one "design" skill that says "do design well." Right: 18 commands each targeting a specific failure (`/audit` for accessibility gaps, `/distill` for over-elaboration, `/colorize` for palette reflex, `/quieter` for over-animation). Superpowers mirrors this at workflow scale.
   - Speaker notes: topic-decomposition creates skills too broad to trigger. Failure-mode decomposition creates skills that fire exactly when needed. Pragmatic threshold: start with one failure mode per skill; split only after the same skill fires for genuinely different failures.
   - Load: balanced
   - Visual: text-only

7. **A chain of enforcement skills beats any single hero skill.**
   - Purpose: explain why users describe Superpowers as transformative rather than additive
   - On-slide: the handoff chain — `using-superpowers` → `brainstorming` → `writing-plans` → `subagent-driven-development` → `executing-plans` → `requesting-code-review` → `finishing-a-development-branch`.
   - Speaker notes: Schwartz's review specifically highlights the chain effect — no individual skill was the thing; the sequence was. Mejba's controlled data backs it: the 14% token reduction was concentrated on complex multi-step tasks where the chain ran end-to-end. On simple tasks, no measurable gain.
   - Load: balanced
   - Visual: horizontal flow diagram — seven labelled nodes, arrows, single accent color on the middle three to mark the execution core

8. **What you give up: bigger files, sharper edges, real authoring effort.**
   - Purpose: the tradeoff slide. A deck without one reads as a sales pitch.
   - On-slide: three honest costs — (1) SKILL.md doubles when you add rationalization tables and ban lists; (2) opinionated skills fight the user's instinct on edge cases; (3) you cannot write a ban list for a domain you have not lived in.
   - Speaker notes: the smallest version of this is still worth doing — one banned phrase, one rebuttal — and costs a twenty-minute authoring session. Address the "sounds heavy" instinct directly; the audience will think it before you say it.
   - Load: balanced
   - Visual: text-only

9. **If this backfires, detect it in 72 hours — not a week.**
   - Purpose: rollback in main flow. This is a recommendation deck (Phase 3d).
   - On-slide: the rollback path + the leading indicator. (a) Keep the prior SKILL.md as `SKILL.md.prev` in the same folder. (b) Track invocation count for 72 hours post-change. (c) If invocations drop more than 30%, triggers regressed — restore, diagnose, retry. Plus three "when not to do this" rules: no enforcement on skills with fewer than five real invocations; no bans without a specific replacement; if enforcement fights user intent, user wins.
   - Speaker notes: anticipate the "I will not notice for a week" objection. The 72-hour invocation-count check is cheap and survives being forgotten.
   - Load: slide-heavy
   - Visual: text-only

10. **One skill. Two moves. Friday.**
    - Purpose: close on the takeaway + the specific ask.
    - On-slide: the takeaway sentence in the largest type, then the action ("Pick one skill. Apply Move 1 and Move 4. Ship by Friday."), then the 72-hour check.
    - Speaker notes: the deck's job is done when the audience commits. Get a name of a skill from at least one person before the room moves on.
    - Load: balanced
    - Visual: text-only
