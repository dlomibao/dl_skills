# Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.

## Audience Model

- **Who's in the room:** Claude Code power users who have already authored at least one production skill and can read frontmatter fluently. Hardest sell: the author of an eight-skill personal catalogue who walks in prepared to decide every line applies to everyone's skills but their own. Second-hardest: the minimalist who treats any ban list as bloat. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they care about:** whether the skill fires when it should, and whether firing changes output in a way they can measure on their own laptop. Token cost surfaces only when pushed. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they already know/believe:** how Claude loads skills, that the `description` field drives invocation, that SKILL.md size eats context. They believe their own skills are fine. They have not separated a skill that *instructs* from a skill that *forbids*. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **Transformation (walk-in → walk-out):** "This is a book review of two popular community skills" → "I know four moves, I can apply the cheapest one this afternoon, and I have a 72-hour check that tells me whether I broke triggering."
- **Likely objections / deep-dive triggers:**
  1. "Why these two and not Anthropic's first-party library?"
  2. "Fourteen rebuttal rows on every invocation costs context. What does that buy?"
  3. "Naming defaults is easy for a visual skill. What does it look like for a testing skill or a docs skill?"
  4. "One person, twelve sessions is thin evidence for a main-flow chart."
  5. "If enforcement tanks triggering, how fast do I find out?"

## Brief

- **Decision/ask:** pick one skill you already own, apply Move 1 (name the specific default you want killed), wire the 72-hour invocation-count check, ship before Friday.
- **Format & time:** 12-minute live talk plus ~5 minutes Q&A. Power-user conference register.
- **Constraints:** 11 main slides plus 5 backup. Self-contained HTML. No corporate template.
- **One-sentence takeaway:** **A skill earns its keep by forbidding the model's specific defaults — not by describing behaviours you wish it had.**
- **Team contributors:** N/A — single-author analysis of two third-party skills.

## Narrative spine

**SCQA + Pyramid (Minto).** Answer up front on slide 1, four supporting moves each with direct evidence from impeccable or superpowers, honest cost, rollback, ask. The four moves are MECE: name defaults / rebut rationalizations / ship a model-free verifier / decompose by failure mode — no overlap, full coverage.

## Cover slide

- **Title:** *Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.*
- **Subtitle (≤12 words):** *Four moves from impeccable and superpowers — for authors of other skills.* (12 words)
- **Meta:** *Power-user talk · 12 min*

Same rhetorical antithesis as v6. Two four-word sentences in one breath. "Forbid" is load-bearing; returns as the middle word of the close. No kicker, no continuation.

## Opening hook (first 30 seconds)

*"The two community skills that changed how I ship stopped telling Claude what to do. One forbids the eleven fonts Claude reaches for by default. The other pre-empts the fourteen excuses Claude uses to skip tests. One of them ships a Node script that grades output with no model in the loop. That is the move — and it is the gap between a skill you wrote for yourself and a skill other people install."*

Spoken, not displayed. Hook and STAR land on the same idea.

## STAR moment

Slide 2, the reframe: both skills look like capability additions and are in fact discipline-enforcement systems wrapped around known model defaults. "Enforcement systems dressed as capability skills" is the hallway-repeatable line.
