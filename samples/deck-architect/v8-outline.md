# Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.

## Audience Model

- **Who's in the room:** Claude Code power users who have already authored at least one production skill and can read frontmatter fluently. Hardest sell: the author of an eight-skill personal catalogue walking in prepared to decide every line applies to everyone else's skills but theirs. Second-hardest: the minimalist who treats any ban list as bloat. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they care about:** whether the skill actually fires when it should, and whether firing changes output in a way they can measure on their own laptop. Token cost is a secondary worry they surface only when pushed. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they already know/believe:** how Claude loads skills; that the `description` field drives invocation; that SKILL.md size eats context. They believe their own skills are fine. They have not yet separated a skill that *instructs* from a skill that *forbids*. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **Transformation (walk-in → walk-out):** "This is a book review of two popular community skills" → "I know four moves, I can apply the cheapest of them this afternoon, and I have a 72-hour check that tells me whether I broke triggering."
- **Likely objections / deep-dive triggers:**
  1. "Why these two and not Anthropic's first-party library?"
  2. "Fourteen rebuttal rows on every invocation costs context. What does that buy?"
  3. "Naming defaults is easy for a visual skill. What does it look like for a testing skill or a docs skill?"
  4. "One person, twelve sessions is thin evidence for a main-flow chart."
  5. "If enforcement tanks triggering, how fast do I find out?"

## Brief

- **Decision/ask:** pick one skill you already own, apply Move 01 (name the specific default you want killed), wire the 72-hour invocation-count check, ship before Friday.
- **Format & time:** 12-minute live talk plus ~5 minutes Q&A. Power-user / late-morning technical conference register.
- **Constraints:** 11 main slides plus 5 backup. Self-contained HTML. No corporate template.
- **One-sentence takeaway:** **A skill earns its keep by forbidding the model's specific defaults — not by describing behaviours you wish it had.**
- **Team contributors:** N/A — single-author analysis of two third-party skills.

## Narrative spine

**SCQA + Pyramid (Minto).** Answer on slide 1, then four supporting moves, each with direct evidence from impeccable or superpowers. Honest cost, rollback, ask. Tool-tour spine would lose the room before the reframe lands on slide 2. The four moves are MECE: naming defaults, rebutting rationalizations, shipping a model-free verifier, and decomposing by failure mode.

Identical shape to v6/v7 — re-stated here so the outline stands alone.

## What changed and why (v8 vs v7)

- **Register reset — fourth distinct physicality in the series.** v5 was cool-teal monitor; v6 was warm-terracotta printed monograph; v7 was chartreuse-on-plum xerox zine. v8 is **indigo-ink-on-oat-paper blueprint ledger under tungsten light** — cool-dark ink on warm-light paper, with a single saturated tungsten-amber accent. The physicality is engineering-ledger / graph-paper, distinct from all three prior registers: not monitor (v5), not printed monograph (v6), not zine (v7).
- **Font register reset.** Display = **Syne** (Bonjour Monde, geometric-grotesque display — distinct from Unbounded/Sora/Bricolage Grotesque/Archivo Narrow). Body = **Spectral** (TypeTogether, screen-tuned serif — distinct from Source Serif 4/Literata/STIX Two Text/Lora/Crimson/Newsreader/Fraunces/Instrument Serif). Mono = **Martian Mono** (Evil Martians/Roboto Mono lineage — distinct from Fira Code/Geist Mono/Azeret Mono/JetBrains Mono/IBM Plex Mono/Space Mono). None shared with v5/v6/v7, none on impeccable's reject list.
- **Palette.** OKLCH neutrals tinted toward oat/warm (hue ~85) for paper; ink tinted toward indigo (hue ~255) at low chroma for legibility. Accent at `oklch(72% 0.160 75)` — tungsten amber, lying in the warm-yellow register distinct from v6's terracotta (hue 42, redder) and v7's chartreuse (hue 118, greener). Accent-ink at `oklch(44% 0.120 60)` so any accent-hued text clears 4.5:1 contrast on oat paper.
- **Verb/cadence unchanged.** v7's cover *Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.* and close *Pick. Forbid. Check.* carry through. The antithesis pair and the bookend-verb are load-bearing content, not register. Changing them would be a different deck.

## Pressure-test log [size: Standard]

**Role adopted:** same as v6/v7 — experienced Claude Code skill author, eight-to-twelve skills in their personal catalogue, six months of active use, mildly defensive. Reading the oat-paper register as "another skin on the same deck" and ready to ask whether the fourth regeneration is telling them anything new.

**Top critiques surfaced, in role:**

1. *"This is the fourth version. What does the palette change buy me?"* → Nothing, and the deck openly says so in the `What changed` section. The register is a test-harness requirement for distinguishability between parallel generations, not a content change. Content and argument are unchanged from v7; only `design-context` and fonts/palette differ.
2. *"If nothing changed in the content, why run the test at all?"* → Because the test is specifically on the scaffold/handoff contract and the documented-assumption block, not on the argument. The question this test answers is whether the skill, followed honestly, reliably produces a clean deck in a new palette without regressing the lint.
3. *"Twelve sessions is still thin n for a main-flow chart."* → Same answer as v7: caption names the n; speaker notes elevate sample-size honesty.
4. *"The amber accent on an oat paper is low-contrast."* → Body text uses `--ink` (L=24) on `--paper` (L=96); the amber is decoration-only on the rendered charts. The text-safe `--accent-ink` variant sits at L=44 with preserved chroma — 52 L-points against paper, clearing the 50-point heuristic. Lint 6e passes on the whitelist; the human-owed contrast check holds.
5. *"Won't `Syne` on the cover read as another generic display face?"* → Syne is explicitly a grotesque-display with a narrow-to-wide optical ladder, not the Inter/DM Sans register. It is not on impeccable's reject list. The test is the same as v5-7: read the cover aloud; does it land as one voice? It does.
