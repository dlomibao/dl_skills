# Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.

## Audience Model

- **Who's in the room:** Claude Code power users who have already authored at least one production skill and can read frontmatter fluently. Hardest sell: the author of an eight-skill personal catalogue who walks in prepared to decide every line of this talk applies to everyone's skills but their own. Second-hardest: the minimalist who treats any ban list as bloat. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they care about:** whether the skill actually fires when it should, and whether firing changes output in a way they can measure on their own laptop. Token cost is a secondary worry they surface only when pushed. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **What they already know/believe:** how Claude loads skills, that the `description` field drives invocation, that SKILL.md size eats context. They believe their own skills are fine. They have not yet separated a skill that *instructs* from a skill that *forbids*. `[INFERRED — confirm]`
- **Transformation (walk-in → walk-out):** "This is a book review of two popular community skills" → "I know four moves, I can apply the cheapest of them this afternoon, and I have a 72-hour check that tells me whether I broke triggering."
- **Likely objections / deep-dive triggers:**
  1. "Why these two and not Anthropic's first-party library?"
  2. "Fourteen rebuttal rows on every invocation costs context. What does that buy?"
  3. "Naming defaults is easy for a visual skill. What does it look like for a testing skill or a docs skill?"
  4. "One person, twelve sessions is thin evidence for a main-flow chart."
  5. "If enforcement tanks triggering, how fast do I find out?"

## Brief

- **Decision/ask:** pick one skill you already own, apply Move 1 (name the specific default you want killed), wire the 72-hour invocation-count check, ship before Friday.
- **Format & time:** 12-minute live talk plus ~5 minutes Q&A. Power-user / late-morning technical conference register.
- **Constraints:** 11 main slides plus 5 backup. Self-contained HTML. No corporate template.
- **One-sentence takeaway:** **A skill earns its keep by forbidding the model's specific defaults — not by describing behaviours you wish it had.**
- **Team contributors:** N/A — single-author analysis of two third-party skills.

## Narrative spine

**SCQA + Pyramid (Minto).** Answer on slide 1, then four supporting moves, each with direct evidence from impeccable or superpowers. Honest cost, rollback, ask. A tool-tour spine would lose the room before the reframe lands on slide 2. The four moves are MECE: naming defaults, rebutting rationalizations, shipping a model-free verifier, and decomposing by failure mode — no overlap, full coverage of what the two reference skills do that generic skills don't.

## Cover slide

- **Title:** *Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.*
- **Subtitle (≤12 words):** *Four moves from impeccable and superpowers — for authors of other skills.* (12 words, same voice, no second argument introduced)
- **Meta:** *Power-user talk · 12 min*

Rhetorical antithesis in two four-word sentences — reads as one complete thought, not three fragments. "Forbid" is the load-bearing verb; the close on slide 11 returns to it (same verb, same rhythm). No kicker eyebrow, no continuation word. The test: read aloud as one breath, it lands as one thing.

## Opening hook (first 30 seconds)

*"The two community skills that changed how I ship stopped telling Claude what to do. One forbids the eleven fonts Claude reaches for by default. The other pre-empts the fourteen excuses Claude uses to skip tests. One of them ships a Node script that grades output with no model in the loop. That is the move — and it is the gap between a skill you wrote for yourself and a skill other people install."*

Spoken, not displayed. Hook and STAR land on the same idea: the best community skills are enforcement systems dressed as capability skills, concretized by the model-free auditor shipping inside a so-called design skill.

## STAR moment

Slide 2, the reframe: both skills look like capability additions and are in fact discipline-enforcement systems wrapped around known model defaults. "Enforcement systems dressed as capability skills" is the hallway-repeatable line.

## What changed and why (v6 vs v5)

- **Cover verb swapped.** v5 was *Great skills refuse. Average skills suggest.* — four+four antithesis with "refuse/suggest". v6 is *Weak skills describe. Strong skills forbid.* — same antithetical shape and length, different verbs. "Forbid" becomes load-bearing: it echoes in the close as the middle word of *Pick. Forbid. Check.* where v5 used *Pick. Ban. Check.* — "forbid" is rhetorically weightier than "ban" and ties tighter back to the cover verb.
- **Font / palette register reset.** v5 used Unbounded (display) + Source Serif 4 (body) + Fira Code (mono), cool pacific-teal accent at hue 202. v6 uses Bricolage Grotesque (display) + Literata (body) + Geist Mono (mono), warm terracotta-rust accent at hue 42. None of v6's fonts are shared with v5. Rationale: v5's cool-teal-on-paper read as instrumentation / monitor. v6's terracotta-on-warm-paper reads as field notebook / printed monograph — same lab-notebook spirit, different physicality. Actively chosen against the reject list (Inter, DM Sans, Space Grotesk, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Newsreader, Lora, Crimson, IBM Plex Sans/Serif/Mono, Outfit) and against v5's picks (Unbounded, Source Serif 4, Fira Code).
- **B2 graph labels deliberately exercise 2.5.2's hyphen wrap.** `using-superpowers`, `requesting-code-review`, and `finishing-a-branch` all exceed the graph node's 16-char soft limit. In 2.5.1 and earlier the graph renderer did not wrap at hyphens, so `requesting-code-review` (22 chars) collided with the neighbouring box. 2.5.2 wraps at hyphens and `render-visual.js` prints non-blocking budget warnings for each wrapped label. v6 keeps the real identifiers (renaming them would lose the point) and documents the wrap as accepted — this is exactly what the budget ladder permits.
- **Subtitle wording adjusted.** v5's subtitle was *Lessons from impeccable and superpowers, for authors of other skills.* (11 words, starts with "Lessons"). v6 is *Four moves from impeccable and superpowers — for authors of other skills.* (12 words, leads with "Four moves" — names the shape of the talk rather than its genre). Same voice, no second argument introduced.
- **Slide 11 verb swapped.** v5 used *Pick. Ban. Check.* — three one-word imperatives. v6 uses *Pick. Forbid. Check.* — same cadence, but the middle word matches the cover verb. The close literally echoes the cover word, tightening the bookend.
- **Waterfall labels shortened.** v5 used *+ Name the default*, *+ Rebut the excuse*, *+ Ship the check*, *+ Split by failure* — all within budget for waterfall slot width. v6 uses *+ Name default*, *+ Rebut excuse*, *+ Ship check*, *+ Split failure* — removes the article to stay comfortably inside the 14-char waterfall budget without relying on wrap. Reads more like lab-notebook shorthand, which matches the new palette register.
